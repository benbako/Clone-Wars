Water is the quietest material we work with. It has no colour of its own, no texture to specify, no supplier to call. It arrives as weather and leaves as weather. In between, it gives a courtyard its geometry.

A still pool doubles a wall. A narrow channel turns a path into a journey. Rain on a metal roof changes the acoustics of an entire house. None of this requires a fountain. Most of the water in our projects is doing something practical: cooling air, watering plants, marking the passage of the day.

## The courtyard as instrument

In Madeira, the terrace gardens are built on a slope that receives fog most mornings. The fog is the site's real water source, but it arrives softly and leaves quickly. We designed a series of shallow terraces with stone lips, planted with moss and ferns, so that the fog condenses and runs into the soil instead of disappearing down the hill.

The geometry is simple: each terrace holds a thin sheet of water after rain. The sheet reflects the sky. For a few hours, the garden has a second horizon. Then it drains, slowly, through the planting.

> A pool is not for looking at. It is for slowing the rain down.

## Sound, not spectacle

Water is also a way to edit sound. In the city, a small courtyard with a basin can mask traffic without a wall. The sound does not need to be loud; it needs to be irregular. A fountain that repeats on a timer becomes noise. Water moving over stone, changing with the wind, stays beneath the threshold of attention.

At Moss Courtyard, the rain garden sits just outside the meeting room window. On wet days, the room fills with a soft, granular sound. People lower their voices. Meetings get shorter. We did not plan that, but we have come to rely on it.

## Designing for drought

A water feature that only works in the rain is a sculpture. A water feature that works in August is infrastructure. Every courtyard we design has a dry season plan: a basin that becomes a gravel garden, a channel that becomes a path, a pool that becomes a place to sit.

This is not a compromise. It is a way of letting the building tell the truth about its climate. In Lisbon, the summer courtyard is pale, dusty and full of bees. In winter, it is dark and reflective. Both versions are the same room.
