About the collective

The Philosophy of Origin

Sylvan Synergy began with a simple observation: the spaces that make us feel most human are rarely the most impressive. They are the ones that let us breathe.

Our manifesto

We believe architecture is a form of care. Not a statement, but a shelter. Not a spectacle, but a habitat.

Our work begins with listening — to the site, the climate, the body — and ends only when a space feels inevitable. This requires patience, close collaboration and a willingness to let the place lead.

Elena Voss & Mateo Ríos
Founding Partners

Origin

A studio grown from a workshop

Sylvan Synergy began in 2009 in a former carpentry workshop in Lisbon, with two architects, a landscape designer and a borrowed kettle. Elena Voss and Mateo Ríos had spent a decade on large cultural projects and wanted to work closer to the ground: houses, gardens and small public rooms.

The first commission was a courtyard renovation for a family who wanted their fig tree to survive a new extension. It did. The studio has been designing around living things ever since.

Today we are twenty-eight people across Lisbon, Kyoto and Vancouver. We still begin every project the same way: a long walk, a notebook, and a conversation about how the client wants to feel at seven in the morning.

Method

Four ways of working

Principles we return to when a drawing gets too clever.

  1. 01

    Listen before drawing

    Every project begins with time on site: light studies, soil tests, conversations, a walk through the neighbourhood at different hours. We do not sketch until we can describe how the place feels.

  2. 02

    Build with what is near

    We source materials and makers within reach of the site wherever possible. Local stone, local timber, local hands. It keeps the work grounded and the carbon low.

  3. 03

    Design for the body

    Comfort is physical. We test ceiling heights, stair depths, door widths and the temperature of a handrail. A beautiful room that is uncomfortable is a drawing, not a building.

  4. 04

    Leave room for time

    We design for the first scratch and the fiftieth season. Planting, patina and repair are part of the plan, not an afterthought.

How we work

Slow architecture, in four movements

  1. 01

    Listen

    Two to four weeks. Site walks, light and wind studies, and conversations with everyone who will use the space.

  2. 02

    Trace

    Four to eight weeks. Sketches, physical models, material samples and a first planting palette.

  3. 03

    Build

    Eight to eighteen months. Collaboration with local makers, regular site visits and one point of contact throughout.

  4. 04

    Tend

    Ongoing. Seasonal reviews, care guides and small adjustments as the building settles into its place.
